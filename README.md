I completed an end-to-end HR Analytics Dashboard in Power BI focused on Employee Attrition & Performance.
Rather than building a dashboard that only reports HR metrics, I wanted to answer questions management teams actually care about:
• Which roles are most at risk of attrition?
• Does training reduce employee turnover?
• How does work-life balance impact retention?
• Are employees and managers aligned on performance expectations?
• Which workforce segments require intervention first?

Key findings from the analysis:
📌 Attrition is heavily concentrated among Sales Representatives(~40%)  and Recruiters(~38%).
📌 Employees consistently rate themselves ~15% higher than managers across departments, highlighting potential calibration and feedback gaps.
📌 Work-Life Balance, Overtime, and Marital Status show strong relationships with attrition outcomes.
📌 Training investment alone does not guarantee retention. Job Satisfaction appears to be a stronger predictor of employee turnover.
📌 Attrition trends have improved over time, suggesting recent workforce initiatives may be delivering positive results.

Insights Derived Through Analysis
1. Training Alone Does Not Guarantee Retention

The scatter analysis shows:
Job roles receiving higher training opportunities still exhibit high attrition.
Some highly trained roles remain among the top attrition groups.

Management Implication:
Training investment without career growth, recognition, workload balance does not improve retention.

2. Job Satisfaction Has Stronger Relationship with Attrition Than Training

The Job Satisfaction vs Attrition visual indicates:
Higher satisfaction tends to align with lower attrition despite similar training exposure.

Management Implication:

Focus on:
➡️ leadership quality
➡️ employee engagement
➡️ career progression
➡️ before increasing training budgets.

3. Overtime Appears to Be an Early Warning Indicator

The dashboard reveals:
Higher overtime groups show elevated attrition.
Departments with heavier overtime also appear among higher-risk segments.

Management Implication:
Overtime monitoring can be used as a proactive attrition risk indicator.

4. Performance And Tenure Are Not Perfectly Correlated
Tenure analysis suggests:
Longer tenure does not automatically translate to top performance ratings.

Management Implication:
Promotion and retention strategies should focus on performance contribution rather than tenure alone.

5. Attrition Trend Is Improving Over Time
The Year-over-Year attrition trend demonstrates a declining trajectory.

Management Implication:
Recent HR initiatives may be positively impacting retention.
Further analysis should identify which interventions contributed most.
